# M2 experiment: BM25 evidence retrieval

**Date:** 2026-10-30 · **Snapshot:** 6 documents, 909 passages (1000 / 200 / 0.5) · **Gold set:** `data/eval/claims.jsonl`, 44 claims

Reproduce with:

```bash
uv run python -m verity evaluate             # headline metrics
uv run python scripts/m2_experiments.py      # everything below (~7 s)
```

**How to read these numbers:**
- The gold set is small (37 answerable claims), and it was written by the same people who built the system.
- One claim moving by one rank can shift MRR by up to 0.027. Differences smaller than that are noise unless the bootstrap interval says otherwise.
- Nothing was tuned on this set. The parameter sweep below is reported for understanding only.

## 1. Setup

| | |
|---|---|
| Retriever | BM25 from scratch with an inverted index (`src/verity/retrieval/bm25.py`); k1 = 1.2, b = 0.75, Lucene idf |
| Analyzer | PDF-artifact repair → NFKC → casefold + accent fold → letter/digit tokens → stopwords → S-stemmer (ADR-011) |
| Claims | 44 total: 22 supported, 10 contradicted, 5 conflicting, 7 insufficient. 29 lexical, 15 paraphrased. 62 evidence quotes |
| Relevance | A passage is relevant if it fully contains a gold evidence quote (ADR-012) |
| Metrics | Recall@k: share of a claim's evidence quotes found in the top k, averaged over claims. MRR: 1/rank of the first relevant passage. Both are computed over the 37 answerable claims |
| Speed | Index build 141 ms; 0.6 ms per query; vocabulary of 6,264 terms |

**Both sides of a dispute.** Of the 5 conflicting claims, the top 5 never held both the supporting and the contradicting evidence. The top 10 did for 1 claim, and the top 20 for 3. **BM25 alone cannot be relied on to surface both sides of a dispute.** This matters most for Verity's goal of avoiding false balance and one-sided answers.

### Before and after completing the gold set

The first evaluation scored MRR **0.611** (R@1 0.459, R@10 0.770). I then inspected the top non-gold passages for every claim whose rank-1 hit was not gold. This is "pooling", the standard TREC method. It showed that the gold set, not the retriever, was wrong in three ways:

1. **Restatements.** NIST states the same finding in its summary and in its chapters with different lead-in words: "WTC 7 withstood debris impact damage…" versus "The building withstood debris impact damage…". Only one wording was in the gold set, so the other copies counted as misses.
2. **A footnote marker inside a sentence.** One copy reads "tall building1 in the design", where the "1" is a footnote marker extracted into the text. This broke the match for a quote copied from the other copy.
3. **Missing direct evidence** for 8 claims. For example, NIST's "The fires on these six floors were fed by combustibles (e.g., desks, chairs, papers, carpet) that were ordinary for commercial occupancies" was ranked 1st for "The fires in WTC 7 burned ordinary office furnishings" but wasn't labelled.

After the fixes (10 claims changed; each change is recorded in the claim's `note`), MRR is **0.736**. The PDF-repair fix described in section 5 changed nothing on the old gold set: MRR was 0.611 both before and after. So the whole improvement comes from better labels. **The first numbers understated BM25 by about 0.12 MRR.**

**Caveat:** pooling from a single system favours that system. Passages BM25 never ranked highly were never reviewed, so a future dense retriever may find relevant passages that the gold set still counts as wrong. Gold quotes should be pooled again from every new retriever (M6).

**Deliberately left out of the gold set** (decision pending, see PROGRESS.md):
- **Reported speech.** Example: Hulsey writing "NIST claims that thermal expansion … push[ed] the girder". The source reports someone else's claim without asserting it.
- **Hypotheses.** Example: NIST §3.2 "The Leading Hypothesis", which was stated before it was tested.

## 3. Reference implementation: rank_bm25

The `okapi` idf variant reproduces `rank_bm25.BM25Okapi` (k1 = 1.5, b = 0.75, ε = 0.25) exactly:

| analyzer | max \|score difference\| over 44 queries × 909 passages | identical top 10 |
|---|---|---|
| raw | 9.8 × 10⁻¹³ | 44 / 44 |
| full | 7.1 × 10⁻¹⁵ | 44 / 44 |

The test suite also checks this parity with Hypothesis on random corpora (`tests/test_bm25.py`), and checks the default variant against the BM25 formula written out by hand.

| configuration | R@1 | R@5 | R@10 | MRR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ours, Lucene idf, k1 = 1.2 (default) | 0.538 | 0.725 | 0.815 | 0.736 |
| ours, Okapi idf, k1 = 1.5 (rank_bm25 settings) | 0.538 | 0.718 | 0.793 | 0.719 |

The rank_bm25 settings score slightly lower: −0.017 MRR, 95% interval [−0.035, −0.003]. The difference is small but consistent.

The two idf formulas behave differently for very common terms. Okapi idf turns negative when a term occurs in more than half the passages, and rank_bm25 replaces that negative value with ε × the mean idf. Here only one term crosses that line: the digit `1`, which is everywhere because of page numbers and footnote markers. Lucene idf is always positive, so the default never needs that floor.

## 4. Analyzer ablations

| configuration | R@1 | R@5 | R@10 | MRR | ΔMRR vs full [95% CI] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| raw (casefold + tokenize) | 0.538 | 0.718 | 0.806 | 0.728 | −0.008 [−0.025, +0.007] |
| + PDF repair | 0.552 | 0.745 | 0.800 | 0.744 | +0.007 [−0.015, +0.041] |
| + repair + stopwords | 0.565 | 0.745 | 0.773 | 0.757 | +0.021 [−0.012, +0.065] |
| **full** (+ S-stemming) | 0.538 | 0.725 | 0.815 | 0.736 | — |
| full without PDF repair | 0.538 | 0.732 | 0.820 | 0.727 | −0.009 [−0.022, +0.000] |
| full without stopwords | 0.579 | 0.752 | 0.829 | 0.758 | +0.021 [−0.023, +0.082] |
| full without stemming | 0.565 | 0.745 | 0.773 | 0.757 | +0.021 [−0.012, +0.065] |

The intervals come from a paired bootstrap over claims (10,000 resamples).

**No analyzer component has an effect we can distinguish from noise on this gold set.** Every interval includes or touches zero. The full analyzer stays the default for principled reasons rather than measured ones:
- PDF repair makes the index match the text a human reads. Without it, "T ower" is indexed as the two terms `t` and `ower`.
- Stemming conflates plurals, which BM25 otherwise treats as unrelated words.

The ablation would only be decisive with a larger gold set.

## 5. PDF extraction artifacts

| document | kerning split | line-break hyphen | spacing diacritic | ligature | page break | "left blank" | running header |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 911-commission-ch9 | 296 | 266 | 0 | 0 | 45 | 0 | 46 |
| bazant-2008 | 2 | 25 | 49 | 218 | 28 | 0 | 0 |
| conversation-2021 (HTML) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| hulsey-2020 | 0 | 6 | 0 | 0 | 124 | 0 | 0 |
| nist-ncstar-1a | 0 | 5 | 0 | 0 | 129 | 13 | 0 |
| schneider-2019 | 0 | 69 | 0 | 0 | 5 | 0 | 0 |

The counts are after the repair fix below, so they only include patterns the repair now acts on.

**Do the artifacts affect retrieval? Yes, per passage. Not measurably in aggregate.**

- **Kerning splits break exact terms.** Without repair, the Commission chapter (the main source on the emergency response) contains "T ower" 231 times and never the word `tower`. Its evidence passages still score through other terms, so the aggregate effect is small. Per evidence document, the mean 1/rank is 0.597 with repair and 0.579 without.
- **Repair can hurt individual queries.** Once "T win T owers" is repaired, `twin` and `tower` match many more Commission passages. That lowers the idf of `tower` and adds competition. For tt-04 the first relevant passage dropped from rank 9 to rank 15 with repair on; for resp-02 it rose from rank 11 to rank 7.
- **Ligatures:**
  - Bažant uses ﬁ, ﬂ, ﬀ and ﬃ 218 times; NFKC handles them.
  - Before NFKC, "suﬃcient" never matched "sufficient".
- **Spacing carons:** "Baˇ zant" becomes `bazant`.
- **Running headers** such as "Final 8-9.5pp 7/17/04 1:24 PM Page 307" add `final`, `pp`, `pm`, `page` and numbers to 46 Commission passages. They make the digit `1` the only term in more than half the passages, and they add noise to queries containing times (resp-01: "9:59", "10:28").
- **Figure and table text.** Bažant's figure axis labels are extracted as text ("Free fall … First 30m of fall … North Tower"). That keyword-dense text ranked 1st to 3rd for resp-02 and 2nd to 3rd for tt-03. 61 passages (6.7%) are at least 25% numbers (figures, tables, tables of contents, references). Such passages make up only 0.9% of top-10 results, so BM25 mostly avoids them, but their keyword-dense label text ("free fall", "tower") is exactly what ranks.

**Bug found and fixed during this investigation.** The first kerning rule joined any detached capital except A and I. That produced false joins on real one-letter labels: "B temperatures" → `btemperature`, "C columns" → `ccolumn`, "B ran" → `bran`. A scan of every candidate in the corpus showed that all real splits involve overhanging capitals (T and Y), which is where typographic kerning applies. The rule now covers T, V, W and Y only, and the corpus has no false joins left (see `m2_experiments.py`, section 5).

## 7. Long-document and document-share effects

NIST NCSTAR 1A provides 39.6% of the passages, so one could worry that it dominates results. It doesn't:

| document | share of all top-10 results | share of passages |
|---|---|---|
| nist-ncstar-1a | 31.4% | 39.6% |
| hulsey-2020 | 15.9% | 23.9% |
| 911-commission-ch9 | 26.1% | 20.5% |
| bazant-2008 | 19.5% | 12.9% |
| conversation-2021 | 5.7% | 1.0% |
| schneider-2019 | 1.4% | 2.2% |

Chunking into passages of at most 1,000 characters removes the document-length effect that whole-document BM25 would have. Each passage competes on its own. The short general-audience article (The Conversation) is over-represented, because its passages state conclusions in plain words that match how claims are phrased.

**Overlap redundancy.** In 25 cases across 44 queries, two adjacent top-10 results were overlapping passages of the same document, which share up to 200 characters. Overlap helps recall at chunk boundaries (17 evidence quotes sit in more than one passage), but it uses up top-k slots on repeated text. A later stage should merge or skip overlapping hits.

## 8. Topic confusion: WTC 7 versus the Twin Towers

| claims about | top-5 hits from a document about the other topic |
|---|---|
| WTC 7 (18 claims) | 3 / 90 |
| Twin Towers (13 claims) | 5 / 65 |

Confusion is low but real. For example, Twin Towers claims about free fall pull in Hulsey's WTC 7 free-fall passages. It matters more for the judge than for retrieval: a passage about WTC 7's free fall must not be cited as evidence about the towers. Each document's `publication_status` states its scope (Bažant: Twin Towers, not WTC 7; Hulsey: WTC 7). The judge (M4) needs that scope in front of it.

## 9. Can BM25 scores detect "insufficient evidence"?

| | top-1 score |
|---|---|
| answerable claims | mean 22.9, min 8.4 |
| insufficient claims | mean 12.4, max 17.1 |
| AUROC | **0.857** |

Separation is moderate, but no threshold works safely. The hardest insufficient claims are the ones that sit close to the corpus's topics:

- **wtc7-15** (insurance settlement; score 13.6): retrieves passages about the lawsuit, but none mention a settlement.
- **tt-13** (EPA air safety; 14.1): retrieves Bažant's use of EPA *dust density* data.
- **none-01** (flight schools; 17.1): retrieves NIST's account of the hijackers flying the planes.

Several answerable claims score lower than these. **A retrieval score measures word overlap, not whether a passage addresses the claim.** INSUFFICIENT has to be decided by the judge (M4) by reading the passages, never by a score threshold.

## 10. Successes and failures

### Successes (relevant evidence at rank 1)

| claim | why it works |
|---|---|
| wtc7-03 "The buckling of Column 79 was the initiating local failure…" | Distinctive shared terms (`column`, `79`, `buckling`, `initiating`) |
| wtc7-05 "WTC 7 was struck by an airplane." (contradicted) | Retrieves "It was not struck by an airplane." BM25 ignores the negation, but the passage is still the right one; deciding the stance is the judge's job |
| wtc7-08 "Part of WTC 7's descent was at free fall for about 2.25 seconds." | Retrieves both NIST and Hulsey, two sources that agree on this measurement |
| wtc7-14 "Experts hired by both sides of an insurance lawsuit studied why Building 7 fell." | Paraphrased, but "lawsuit" and "insurance" are rare terms |
| tt-02 "Allegations of controlled demolition of the twin towers have scientific merit." (contradicted) | Distinctive phrase ("controlled demolition") |

### Failures (no relevant passage in the top 10)

| claim | first relevant rank | cause |
|---|---|---|
| wtc7-02 "Building 7 came down because blazes inside it weakened the structure." | 126 | **Vocabulary mismatch.** The evidence says "collapsed due to uncontrolled fires"; the claim says "came down", "blazes" and "weakened". Only `building` and `7` match. |
| wtc7-07 "Explosive charges were used to demolish Building 7." | 12 | **Topical but not conclusive passages rank first.** NIST's description of *how it simulated* blast scenarios mentions "explosive charge" many times and takes ranks 1–3. The conclusion ("no blast event played a role") uses different words. |
| tt-03 "The twin towers came down at essentially free-fall speed." | 22 | **Repaired kerning creates a distractor plus figure text.** The Commission's background passage ("T win T owers came to occupy…") matches `twin`, `came` and `essentially`, and Bažant's figure labels match `free fall`. |
| tt-04 "Once the top of a tower began moving down, the structure below could not have stopped the collapse." | 15 | **Paraphrase of technical language.** Bažant writes "inevitability of gravity driven progressive collapse"; only `collapse` overlaps. |

All four are paraphrased claims. Three of the four involve the most contested questions (the cause of WTC 7's collapse, demolition, free fall). Lexical retrieval is weakest exactly where Verity needs it most.

## 11. Limitations

- **Small, self-built gold set:**
  - 37 answerable claims, written by the system's builders and completed by pooling from a single system.
  - Conflicting claims (n = 5) and the "other" topic (n = 1) are too small to interpret.
- **Relevance = quote containment.** A passage that says the same thing in other words is unjudged and counts as irrelevant. Pooling reduced this but didn't eliminate it.
- **Evidence-level recall treats every quote as equally necessary,** including partial quotes for compound claims (resp-01).
- **BM25 ignores negation and word order.** "was not struck" and "was struck" score identically. That is correct for retrieval but must never be used as a stance signal.
- **Search-time repairs are heuristics** tuned on this corpus's extractor (pypdf). Another extractor or document may produce different artifacts.
- **Not tested:** query expansion, phrase or proximity matching, BM25F field weighting, and metadata filtering by document scope.

## 12. Recommendations (ranked by expected value)

1. **Add dense and hybrid retrieval (M6) before anything else for recall.** The failures are paraphrase and vocabulary mismatch, which word-overlap methods can't fix. Re-pool the gold set with the new system.
2. **Retrieve deeper and diversify before judging.** Pass more than the top 5 to the judge, and/or select evidence per source document, so both sides of a conflicting claim reach the judge (0/5 conflicting claims had both sides in the top 5).
3. **Merge overlapping passages** in the result list (25 adjacent duplicates across 44 queries).
4. **Strip known boilerplate at index time,** behind a versioned, per-document rule: running headers, "intentionally left blank", and figure-label blocks. Stored text stays unchanged (ADR-010/011); only the index view changes.
5. **Grow the gold set to 100+ claims,** with more conflicting claims and a second annotator. Report inter-annotator agreement, and decide the reported-speech and hypothesis policy first.
6. **Never use a BM25 score threshold to declare INSUFFICIENT** (AUROC 0.86 with overlapping ranges). That decision belongs to the judge and verifier.
