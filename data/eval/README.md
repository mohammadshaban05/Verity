# Gold set

`claims.jsonl` holds 50 claims for evaluating retrieval (and, later, judging) against the seed corpus in [`../corpus`](../corpus). It is generated by `scripts/build_gold.py`, which records how every claim was made. Labelling rules are in ADR-012.

```json
{"id": "fever-10", "claim": "Phillip Glass has written eleven concertos.", "label": "contradicted",
 "topic": "music", "origin": "fever:shared_task_dev:165226",
 "evidence": [{"doc_id": "wiki-philip-glass", "quote": "15 symphonies, 12 concertos, nine string quartets", "stance": "contradicts"}],
 "note": "FEVER label SUPPORTS (2017 text). The pinned 2026 revision says 12 concertos; ..."}
```

- **Quotes are verbatim** from the corpus text; whitespace may differ. A passage is relevant if it fully contains a quote.
- **The label follows from the stances.** Every quote supports → supported; every quote contradicts → contradicted; both → conflicting; no quote → insufficient. The loader rejects any other combination.
- **Labels describe what the texts say, not what is true.** `verity-02` is "conflicting" because two encyclopedias give 66 and 65 million years. It does not say which date is right.
- **`origin`** is `<dataset>:<split>:<id>` for adapted claims and `verity` for claims written for this project.

## Composition

| origin | supported | contradicted | conflicting | insufficient | tests |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| FEVER dev | 9 | 10 | – | 4 | everyday facts; definitions; misleading word overlap |
| SciFact dev | 6 | 5 | – | 5 | expert paraphrase; on-topic but non-addressing abstracts |
| WikiContradict | – | – | 8 | – | both sides of a contradiction inside one article |
| Verity | 1 | – | 2 | – | agreement and conflict between two separately written sources |

## How the claims were adapted

- **SciFact** (16 claims). These are a seeded, label-stratified sample of the dev split, drawn with the same seed as the corpus selection. Each annotated rationale sentence becomes one quote, and the stance follows SciFact's label. For NEI claims, the cited abstracts are in the corpus but do not address the claim.
- **FEVER** (23 claims). These are dev-set claims about nine pinned articles. FEVER's evidence points into a 2017 Wikipedia dump, so every claim was **re-checked by hand against the pinned 2026 revision**, and the quotes and labels are ours. Four labels differ from FEVER's; each claim's `note` gives FEVER's label and the reason:

  | claim | FEVER | here | why |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | fever-04 "…had several children including Mary and Arthur." | NOT ENOUGH INFO | supported | FEVER used only lead sections; the full article names them |
  | fever-10 "…has written eleven concertos." | SUPPORTS | contradicted | the 2026 revision says 12 concertos |
  | fever-15 "Yandex operates in Belgium." | REFUTES | insufficient | neither Yandex article mentions Belgium; FEVER inferred it from a country list |
  | fever-20 "Aerobic exercise lowers resting heart rate in the long term." | SUPPORTS | insufficient | the 2026 revision no longer states it |

- **WikiContradict** (8 claims). Each claim is written so that one passage supports it and the other contradicts it. WikiContradict's own context passages are sometimes paraphrased or taken from infoboxes. We therefore pinned each article to a revision in which both sides appear in the prose, and copied the quotes verbatim from it. Articles where this was not possible were dropped.
- **Verity** (3 claims). These test cross-source agreement (dodo extinction causes) and cross-source conflict (Yandex's launch year; the end of the Cretaceous), using the English and Simple English articles.
- **Pooling.** After the first evaluation, the top-ranked passages that were not yet gold were reviewed for every claim whose rank-1 hit was not gold. Direct evidence was added to 5 claims, each marked "Gold extended by pooling." in its `note`. Reported speech, hypotheses and evidence that needs an inference step are not added.

Phrasing is not annotated. `evaluate` measures it: a claim is "lexical" if at least half of its index terms occur in its closest quote, otherwise "paraphrase".
